A matrix looks like a table of numbers, but it is really a move you make to space, and its columns record where the axes land. Starting from two arrows on a flat grid, we see why matrix multiplication is one move after another, why a zero determinant crushes space and can't be undone, what eigenvectors are, and why every matrix is a rotation, a stretch, and a rotation (the singular value decomposition). Along the way: video game graphics, shadows, neural networks, Google's PageRank, and image compression. A visual linear algebra guide for students.
