# Aion: Audience Retention Playbook

22 rules to keep viewers watching to the end.

## Opening

- **1. Question first.** State the title's promise within 10 seconds, then show the visuals as its answer.
- **2. Map in one sentence.** "We'll start with one arrow and end up rotating 3D space." No table of contents.
- **3. Preview the result.** Show it unexplained: "by the end, you'll see why."
- **4. Wrong intuition first.** "You'd think X… but watch."

## Body

- **5. Keep the screen moving.** Something changes every few seconds; restate the reason to watch at each new idea. Trim long `self.wait()` calls.
- **6. Show progress.** Keep earlier results in a corner; label steps (graphical progress)
- **7. One or two open loops.** Open the next question before closing the current one.
- **8. Plant "this matters later."** Then pay it off.
- **9. Transform, don't cut.** Morph the same object across scenes for aha moments.
- **10. Switch mode every 60–90 s.** Derivation, visual, application, prediction, 2D to 3D.
- **11. Re-hook at the midpoint.** A new surprise or a reminder of the big question.

## Clarity

- **12. One new idea at a time.** Define symbols first; give each concept a beat to sink in.
- **13. Concrete before general.** A specific example, then the formula.
- **14. Guide the eye.** One focal point, fixed color meanings, minimal on-screen text.
- **15. Pause and predict.** Let viewers guess before the reveal.

## Chapters and ending

- **16. Small payoff per chapter, big payoff at the end.**
- **17. Close each chapter** with a 5–10 s visual callback, not a spoken recap.
- **18. Keep every promise.** Track them in the script.
- **19. End on the payoff, not a recap.** Then point to one next video.

## Production and review

- **20. Write for the ear.** Short sentences, no filler; read it aloud.
- **21. Audio first.** Clear narration; quiet or no music under dense parts.
- **22. Cut to length.** Drop any scene that doesn't serve the main question.
