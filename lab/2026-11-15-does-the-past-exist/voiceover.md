# FORM

Think of a moment you remember. A place. The light. The people who were there.

It felt solid. Real.

And then, it was gone.

Does the past still exist?

# FLOW

We live inside a single moving instant. Each moment arrives, and slips behind us, like the walls of an endless tunnel.

We call that motion time. And what it leaves behind, we call the past.

# MEMORY

But where does it go?

We keep pieces of it in memory. Yet memories are not recordings. Each time we remember, the mind rebuilds the scene, a little differently.

Fragments fade. Edges blur.

# LIGHT

And yet, the universe keeps its own records.

The sunlight on your skin left the Sun eight minutes ago. The light of the Andromeda galaxy set out two and a half million years ago.

To look out is to look back. The past is still arriving.

# LAYERS

Physics goes further.

In Einstein's relativity, there is no single, universal now. What is now for one observer can be past, or future, for another.

So some physicists picture spacetime as a block: past, present and future, all laid out together. Nothing disappears. Every moment simply is.

When his friend Michele Besso died, Einstein wrote that the distinction between past, present and future is only a stubbornly persistent illusion.

# DOUBT

But not everyone agrees.

Perhaps only the present is real, and the past survives only in its traces.

Perhaps the past is fixed, and the future is still being written.

Or perhaps every moment exists, and the passing is something we feel.

Physics has not decided.

# END

The moment you just lived is already behind you. Its light is still travelling. Its traces are scattered through the world.

So, if the past no longer exists...

Where did it go?
